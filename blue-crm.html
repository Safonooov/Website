Overview

Blue CRM is an enterprise-grade e-commerce operational ecosystem designed to help teams manage fast-moving orders, customer context, warehouse workload, support conversations, product data, and operational planning in one structured workspace.

The product challenge was not simply to display more data. E-commerce operators already deal with too much fragmented information. The goal was to reduce cognitive load, surface operational blockers earlier, and make warehouse-to-customer workflows easier to scan and act on.

Scope

Product Design, Design System, Information Architecture

Surfaces

Dashboard · Orders · Products · Customers · Calendar · Messaging

Problem

The problem

E-commerce operators manage hundreds of fast-moving orders across fragmented tools, causing extreme cognitive overload. Critical operational blockers — such as warehouse backlog, delayed handovers, refund requests, system alerts, and stock-outs — can get buried under dense, unprioritized data noise, directly impacting fulfillment speed and customer satisfaction.

  • 01

    Operations managers need to understand the current order and warehouse state quickly.

  • 02

    Support agents need customer and order context next to communication.

  • 03

    Product teams need clean product data management without losing operational visibility.

  • 04

    Managers need alerts and dashboards that support action, not decorative reporting.

  • 05

    Teams need consistent patterns across orders, products, customers, calendar, and messaging.

Discovery

Discovery

The discovery phase focused on mapping the operational flow from warehouse workload to customer communication. Instead of treating the CRM as a static database, I looked at how operators move through daily work: monitoring order status, identifying bottlenecks, reacting to stock issues, coordinating handovers, updating product data, and resolving customer requests.

Jobs-to-be-done

  • —Operations managers need to see blockers before they become fulfillment delays.
  • —Warehouse and fulfillment teams need order status and backlog signals to be easy to scan.
  • —Support agents need customer telemetry, active orders, order history, and files next to the conversation.
  • —Product managers need structured product editing with media, supplier data, categories, and variations.
  • —Managers need planning tools for meetings, campaigns, shipments, and operational deadlines.

This helped frame Blue CRM as an operational workspace rather than a simple contact database. The product needed to connect dashboard monitoring, order work, product management, customer support, calendar planning, and messaging into one system.

Competitive analysis

Competitive analysis

I reviewed CRM, e-commerce admin, order management, warehouse operations, and support tooling patterns to understand how complex operational data is usually organized. The recurring problem was not a lack of information, but weak prioritization: many tools show the data, but do not clearly separate urgent blockers from everyday operational context.

What I looked for

  • —How e-commerce dashboards surface order and fulfillment status.
  • —How warehouse tools expose backlog and bottlenecks.
  • —How CRMs structure customer profiles and order history.
  • —How support tools keep communication connected to customer data.
  • —How product management tools handle product attributes, media, and variations.
  • —How calendars and planning tools represent operational deadlines.
  • —How dense tables stay scannable under high data volume.
  • —How alert systems separate urgent issues from regular dashboard metrics.

This shaped the product direction: Blue CRM should not become another dense admin panel. The interface needed a predictable layout system, strong information hierarchy, and clear separation between action, monitoring, and context.

Product strategy

Product strategy

01

Proportional Live Funnel

Conventional bar charts can be too abstract for live operations. I replaced them with a proportional status funnel that instantly visualizes warehouse workload and current order backlog, helping managers spot fulfillment bottlenecks quickly.

02

Proactive Triage / Action Center

I positioned a high-priority Action Center at the top of the workspace to separate critical operational threats — such as critical issues, refunds, stock-outs, and system alerts — from day-to-day metrics. The goal was to make urgent issues impossible to miss.

03

Context-First Messaging

The communications hub was structured around context-first support. Customer details, lifetime value, total orders, last order, tags, files, and internal notes sit next to the conversation, so support agents can resolve issues without switching tabs.

04

Systematic Layout Integrity

I applied a rigorous 8px layout grid, predictable left-aligned table architecture, soft card surfaces, and a muted Slate Blue analytical color scheme. High-intensity blue is reserved for primary actions, while the rest of the UI stays calm and scannable.

Hypotheses

Product hypotheses

H1

If critical operational alerts are separated from daily metrics, managers can respond to high-risk issues faster.

Design response

Action Center positioned at the top of the dashboard for critical issues, refunds, stock-outs, and system alerts.

H2

If warehouse workload is shown as a proportional live funnel, managers can understand backlog distribution faster than with abstract charts.

Design response

Status funnel that shows live order stages such as New, Assembly, Ready for Shipping, and Handed to Delivery.

H3

If recent orders are displayed in a predictable, scannable table, operators can prioritize fulfillment and exceptions faster.

Design response

Recent Orders table with order ID, customer, date, total, payment state, fulfillment status, and row actions.

H4

If order details combine fulfillment status, item list, payment summary, customer context, and address data, operators can complete fulfillment actions without switching screens.

Design response

Order detail page with fulfillment timeline, item breakdown, payment summary, customer card, address, and primary action.

H5

If product editing keeps attributes, supplier data, categories, dimensions, media, and variations in one structured layout, teams can maintain catalog data with fewer mistakes.

Design response

Product detail editor with grouped sections, media upload, supplier metadata, categories, and variation management.

H6

If customer telemetry is visible next to messaging, support agents can resolve issues without switching tabs.

Design response

Three-panel messaging layout with inbox, conversation, and customer context panel.

H7

If calendar planning uses the same visual system as the rest of the product, operations, shipments, campaigns, and meetings feel connected.

Design response

Calendar page with month/week/day controls, calendar categories, upcoming events, and operational deadlines.

Operational dashboard

Action first, analytics second.

The Action Center sits at the top of the workspace and isolates high-priority issues such as critical issues, refunds, stock-outs, and system alerts. Below it, order and support sections provide operational context without competing with urgent blockers.

The dashboard separates urgent operational blockers from daily metrics, helping teams understand what needs attention first.

Design decision Action first, analytics second.

Scannable order operations

Dense table, low visual noise, clear status hierarchy.

The order table is designed for fast triage. Operators can scan order ID, customer, date, total, payment status, fulfillment status, and row actions without opening every order.

Recent orders use predictable alignment and muted statuses to keep fast-moving operational data scannable.

Design decision Dense table, low visual noise, clear status hierarchy.

Structured product management

Structured editing instead of a long unprioritized form.

Product data can become messy quickly in e-commerce operations. The editor keeps catalog attributes grouped into sections and uses consistent form patterns, chips, upload zones, and media previews.

Product editing groups supplier data, categories, dimensions, media, and variations into a predictable structure.

Design decision Structured editing instead of a long unprioritized form.

Order detail and fulfillment

Bring order execution and customer context into one screen.

The fulfillment timeline makes the order state visible at the top. The operator can inspect items, payment state, customer information, addresses, and activity without switching between tools.

Order detail combines fulfillment status, item breakdown, payment summary, customer context, and delivery data in one workspace.

Design decision Bring order execution and customer context into one screen.

Contextual order creation

Reduce mistakes by keeping the order summary visible.

Creating a manual order requires multiple decisions. The layout separates the main form from a persistent summary panel, so operators can review totals, payment method, and notes while building the order.

Create Order keeps customer selection, products, shipping, payment method, and summary visible during the same flow.

Design decision Reduce mistakes by keeping the order summary visible.

Operational team profiles

Operational visibility extends beyond customers and orders.

Blue CRM also supports internal operations. The manager profile gives visibility into earnings, orders handled, plan progress, performance, role, schedule, team members, and day-off balance.

Team profiles combine performance, payment history, schedule, contact data, and role context in one place.

Design decision Operational visibility extends beyond customers and orders.

Contextual creation flows

Short focused modal instead of full-page navigation.

The Add New Customer modal supports individual/business profile type, contact data, country, segment, tags, welcome email, and marketing subscription while keeping the underlying customer context visible.

Creation flows stay focused and modal-based, letting users add records without losing their current workspace.

Design decision Short focused modal instead of full-page navigation.

Operational planning

Planning is part of the operational system, not a separate tool.

The calendar gives teams a shared view of operational events: standups, shipments, campaign launches, warehouse inventory, planning deadlines, and reviews.

Calendar planning reuses the same visual system to connect meetings, campaigns, shipments, and deadlines.

Design decision Planning is part of the operational system, not a separate tool.

Context-first messaging

Support conversations are connected to operational customer data.

The messaging layout uses three panels: inbox, conversation, and customer context. This reduces tab-switching by keeping lifetime value, total orders, last order, tags, shared files, and internal notes visible.

Support agents can resolve issues with customer telemetry, tags, files, and order context next to the conversation.

Design decision Support conversations are connected to operational customer data.

Design system

Reducing visual noise in a data-heavy product

The UI system was designed to reduce visual noise in a data-heavy operational product. I used a muted Slate Blue analytical palette, strict 8px spacing logic, left-aligned table architecture, soft card surfaces, and restrained use of high-intensity blue for primary actions.

8px grid Muted Slate Blue palette Brand blue reserved for primary CTAs Soft white / light gray surfaces Consistent sidebar Card and panel system Dense but scannable tables Status labels and pills Form fields and modals Charts and funnel patterns Calendar categories Messaging panels Typography and spacing

The design system had to work across very different surfaces — dashboards, order tables, product forms, order detail pages, customer profiles, modals, calendars, and messaging — without each section feeling like a separate product.

Outcome

Outcome

Blue CRM is a live product used by real users. The final design defines an enterprise-grade e-commerce operations workspace that connects order visibility, warehouse workload, product data, customer context, support communication, calendar planning, and daily operational actions in one system.

The main product decision was to reduce cognitive load by separating urgent alerts from analytical context and keeping customer and order information close to the workflows where decisions happen.

The result is a more structured operational interface where teams can monitor issues, manage orders, update products, support customers, and plan work without constantly switching between disconnected tools.
