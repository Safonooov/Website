Crowdcruit

AI hiring platform for recruiters, candidates, and companies.

Role

Product Designer

Platform

Web app

Live · Generating revenue

Overview

Crowdcruit started as an MVP challenge: define the core product experience for a three-sided AI hiring platform and bring it to production. The goal was to move beyond static screens and design the workflows required for a usable, revenue-generating product.

Crowdcruit is built around AI-generated candidate-role matches. The challenge was not only to show a match score, but to design the workflows around it: recruiters need to understand and propose candidates, candidates need control over profile access, and companies need a clear way to review, interview, and give feedback.

Scope

MVP design, UX flows, UI system, visual identity

Users

Recruiters · Candidates · Companies

Problem

The problem

Hiring workflows are often fragmented across sourcing tools, spreadsheets, candidate databases, calendars, and feedback forms. AI matching can generate recommendations, but without clear reasoning, consent, and next actions, those recommendations are hard to trust or act on.

  • 01

    Recruiters need an actionable workspace for candidate-role matches.

  • 02

    Candidates need visibility and control over who can access their profile.

  • 03

    Companies need a focused review and feedback workflow.

Approach

Designing a three-sided workflow

Recruiter match Candidate access Company review Interview Feedback

I structured the MVP around three role-specific workspaces instead of one generic hiring dashboard. Each workspace uses the same visual system and interaction patterns, but the content and actions are specific to the user's role.

Recruiter workspace

Finds and proposes candidate-role matches.

Candidate workspace

Controls which companies can access the profile.

Company workspace

Reviews candidates, requests interviews, and gives feedback.

Recruiter — match detail

Candidate — access request

Company — candidate proposal

Instead of designing one generic dashboard, I separated the experience into three workspaces with shared visual patterns and role-specific actions.

Recruiter workspace

Turning AI matches into proposals

The recruiter experience is centered on one object: a candidate-role match. The interface combines candidate context, role context, match score, readiness, risks, and the next best action.

Match detail gives the recruiter a focused view of the candidate-role fit, risks, readiness, and the next best action.

The main screen keeps the match understandable: who the candidate is, what role they match with, why the score is high, and what might block the proposal. The right-side panel is reserved for contextual actions instead of overloading the list or the header.

The proposal flow lives in the right panel, keeping the recruiter in context while preparing the candidate pitch.

The proposal flow combines readiness checks, the recipient, an editable recruiter pitch, and the items included in the proposal. This keeps the workflow lightweight while still making the recruiter accountable for the final message.

After sending, the interface returns to an updated state instead of resetting the workflow.

Completed actions update the match state and change the next best action. This pattern helps the recruiter understand what already happened and what should happen next.

Scheduling follows the same contextual panel pattern: participants, suggested slots, meeting room, and invite message stay attached to the match.

Scheduling reuses the contextual panel — interview type, participants, suggested and custom time slots, the meeting room, and an invite message — all without leaving the match.

Scheduled interviews collect into one list view, with confirmation and feedback states.

Candidate workspace

Profile access stays with the candidate

One of the key MVP decisions was to avoid treating candidates as passive database records. Before a company can review a full profile, the candidate sees the opportunity, the company, the match, and the exact profile items requested.

The candidate sees the opportunity and the access request before anything is shared.

The candidate can evaluate the role and company before approving access. The page explains the match, salary range, work mode, and what the company wants to review.

The access review panel makes profile sharing explicit and configurable.

The request panel shows what will be shared: profile summary, verified skills, CV, availability, and salary expectations. The candidate can approve access, decline the request, or adjust the shared items.

Success states confirm exactly what changed and what happens next.

After approval, the system confirms the company, role, shared items, and time of the action. This makes the privacy state explicit instead of hidden.

Access is not a one-time decision. The candidate can manage or revoke it later.

The manage access state reinforces trust: candidates stay in control even after approving a request.

Secondary states cover declining or archiving without turning the flow into a heavy settings experience.

Company workspace

Reviewing candidates and closing the loop

The company side focuses on hiring decisions. Instead of showing a raw database profile, the interface frames each candidate as a proposal for a specific role.

The company receives a focused candidate proposal, not a raw profile.

The proposal detail answers the company's main question: should we move this candidate forward? It shows the candidate, role, match score, fit reasoning, verified signals, risks, and available actions.

Feedback is structured enough to be useful, but lightweight enough to complete quickly.

The feedback panel captures an overall decision, skill-level feedback, a team note, and the recommended next step. This turns subjective discussion into a structured hiring signal.

Submitted feedback updates the candidate state and gives the company a clear next action.

The success state closes the loop and gives the hiring team a clear follow-up, such as viewing status or requesting an interview.

Role setup

Role management and setup

The company workspace also needed a simple way to manage open roles and create new ones without becoming a full ATS.

Open roles are displayed as operational rows: proposed candidates, interviews, feedback, and status.

The roles page avoids heavy analytics and focuses on which roles need attention. Each row gives the hiring team enough context to open the role and continue work.

Creating a role happens in the same right-panel system, keeping the company in context.

The create role flow starts with the essentials: title, team, work mode, location, seniority, employment type, and salary range. The same panel pattern can later extend into requirements and scorecard review.

Design system

One system for three users

The visual system had to support three different workspaces without feeling like three separate products. I used a shared dark interface language, restrained green accents, thin card borders, compact status patterns, and contextual right-side panels across the MVP.

Dark premium interface Green action & success accent Right-side contextual panels Thin bordered cards Status dots & pills Consistent tabs Compact data rows Avatars & company marks Typography & spacing system

I also explored the visual identity direction, including the Crowdcruit mark, green accent system, typography direction, and dark interface language. The identity was kept minimal so the product workflows could stay the focus.

Outcome

Outcome

The MVP was launched to production and is currently used as a working, revenue-generating product. The final design connected the core workflows across three user roles: recruiters can act on AI-generated matches, candidates can control profile access, and companies can review candidates, request interviews, and submit feedback.

The main design decision was to keep complex workflows inside contextual right-side panels, so users can take action without losing the surrounding context.

As a product design project, Crowdcruit helped define the information architecture, interaction patterns, and visual system for a multi-sided AI hiring product that moved from MVP design into a live product.

Focus

What I focused on

  • Designing AI recommendations as explainable, actionable workflows
  • Giving candidates control over profile visibility
  • Keeping three user roles visually consistent but functionally distinct
  • Using contextual panels for complex actions
  • Balancing dense hiring data with a calm, premium interface
  • Defining an MVP scope that could ship to production

In closing

Crowdcruit shows how AI matching, privacy, and hiring collaboration can work as one connected product system.
